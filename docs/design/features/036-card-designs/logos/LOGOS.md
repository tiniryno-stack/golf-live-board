# Course logos — reference for passport stamps

**Reference only.** These are each club's own logos, collected so a designer can redraw the idea as a
simple one-color stamp emblem. They are not shipped in the app and must not be.

Collected Nov 24, 2026, in a browser, mostly from each club's own website (exceptions are named in each
section). Management-company, hotel-brand, city-seal and tee-time-site logos were skipped. 68 courses in four groups:

1. **Courses the group has played** (from the live database): Twin Creeks, Bridlewood, Firewheel Lakes.
2. **Best-known DFW courses (20):** famous public, municipal, resort and tour-host clubs, some private.
3. **More DFW courses (30), public first:** every one is public, municipal or semi-private except Lantana
   (private; found out during collection).
4. **Austin area (15):** 5 best-known + 10 more, public first.

"Most popular" is a hand-picked list of well-known clubs, not a ranking from any one source.
Files are `<slug>.<ext>`; extra versions are `<slug>-<variant>.<ext>`. Several are **white or off-white on
transparent**: open them on a dark background.

**Watch-outs for the designer:**
- **Pro team logos:** Cowboys Golf Club and Texas Rangers Golf Club use pro sports team marks under license (the
  NFL star, the MLB "T"). Don't copy those closely; draw something of the course instead.
- **No picture to work from:** Tierra Verde, Austin Country Club, Riverchase, Tangle Ridge and Hancock are lettering
  only, and TPC Craig Ranch only has the TPC network badge. Their stamps need a motif from the club's landmarks,
  listed in each section.
- **Shared mark:** Roy Kizer and Jimmy Clay are one 36-hole complex with one logo.
- **Rough source art:** the four City of Austin courses (Kizer, Clay, Morris Williams, Hancock) only exist as
  scorecard scans.

## The Golf Club at Twin Creeks — Allen, TX

- **Files:**
  - `twin-creeks.svg` — the crest from the site's top navigation (vector, ~87 × 96 at its native size, scales cleanly).
  - `twin-creeks-monogram.svg` — the "TC" monogram in a square, from the mobile header.
  - Both are **white artwork on a transparent background** (the site sets them on a dark navy header), so they look
    blank in a light file viewer. Open them on a dark background.
- **Source page:** https://arcisgolf.com/clubs/the-golf-club-at-twin-creeks/home (twincreeksgolf.com redirects here).
  File URLs: https://arcisgolf.com/hubfs/Top%20Nav-Club%20at%20Twin%20Creek%20Logo-2.svg and
  https://arcisgolf.com/hubfs/TwinCreeksMobile.svg. The club's site is hosted by its management company (Arcis),
  but these are the club's own marks, not the Arcis castle-tower logo, which sits next to them in the footer and was skipped.
- **What the emblem is:** an upright oval crest. In the middle is one large, full, spreading oak-like tree drawn in
  fine detail: thick trunk, wide round canopy, roots and a strip of ground at the base. "THE GOLF CLUB" curves
  along the top of the oval border; a ribbon banner across the bottom reads "TWIN CREEKS". No creek, water or
  golf imagery in it. The second mark is just the letters "TC" in a serif face inside a square (lettering only).
- **The one thing to draw:** the single spreading oak tree.
- **Colors:** single color: white (the SVG fills are all `#fff`), used on the site's dark navy.
- **How sure:** **sure.** It is the logo in the header and footer of the club's own site, and the file names say
  "Club at Twin Creek Logo".
- **Club's own symbols / landmarks:** the site calls it an "Arnold Palmer Signature Golf Course" and talks about
  "five stunning lakes, two natural creeks, mature trees" and rolling fairways (the two creeks are the name).
  It also mentions The Taproom restaurant and an Airstream outdoor bar. No named tree or signature hole is called out.

## Bridlewood Golf Club — Flower Mound, TX

- **Files:**
  - `bridlewood.png` — the full logo, 1120 × 600, **white on transparent** (open on a dark background).
  - `bridlewood-color-icon.png` — the site's 180 × 180 app icon: the same logo in its real color on white.
- **Source page:** https://www.bridlewoodgolf.com/ . File URLs:
  https://www.bridlewoodgolf.com/images/logo/logo-brand.png (listed in the homepage's code; the header shows a
  200-px copy, `/images/logo/logo-white-no-shadow.png`) and https://www.bridlewoodgolf.com/images/logo/apple-touch-icon.png
- **What the emblem is:** a horse's head in profile facing right, drawn in a loose, brushy style: the mane streams
  back to the left in long ragged brush strokes, like wind. A thin golf flagstick runs diagonally across the head,
  with its little flag at the top near the ears. A sweeping curved line under the head reads like a hill or water.
  Below it, "BRIDLEWOOD" in a tall, slightly medieval display face, and "GOLF CLUB" smaller underneath.
- **The one thing to draw:** the horse's head (with the flagstick across it if it still reads at tiny size).
- **Colors:** one color: a deep blue-teal, about `#083850` (sampled from the icon); white version for dark backgrounds.
- **How sure:** **sure.** It is the logo in the header of the club's own site and its home screen icon.
- **Club's own symbols / landmarks:** the site leads with "rolling terrain, mature hardwoods, and scenic water
  features" and says the 7,111-yard course was designed by PGA Tour veteran D.A. Weibring. The horse ties to the
  name (a bridle, Flower Mound's horse-country roots); the site does not name a signature hole or landmark.

## Firewheel Golf Park — Garland, TX (Lakes course)

- **Files:**
  - `firewheel.png` — the round badge, 443 × 451, full color on white. Saved from Google Images' copy of the image
    on the club's Facebook page ("Firewheel Golf Park | Garland TX"); the original on Facebook needs a login.
  - `firewheel-site-header.png` — the logo actually shown on the club's website, 175 × 175 canvas with a small
    horizontal lock-up (flower + "FIREWHEEL GOLF PARK"). Small, but it is the official site's own file.
- **Source pages:** https://www.golffirewheel.com/ (site header, file
  https://www.golffirewheel.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8098/2021/04/Untitled-design-65.png) and
  https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Firewheel+Golf+Park%22+Garland+golf+logo&udm=2 (first result, from Facebook).
  The site's "Powered by GolfNow" logo was skipped.
- **What the emblem is:** a firewheel wildflower (Indian blanket / gaillardia) seen face-on: a ring of petals that
  are red-orange at the center and yellow at the jagged tips, outlined in black, around a dark patterned center,
  with green leaves behind it and a short curvy stem. On the badge, "FIREWHEEL" arcs over the top and "GOLF PARK"
  arcs under it in green block letters. The website version puts the same flower to the left of the words.
  Same flower for all of Firewheel's courses; there is no separate Lakes course logo.
- **The one thing to draw:** the firewheel flower, face-on.
- **Colors:** red-orange and yellow petals, black outlines and center detail, green leaves, stem and lettering.
- **How sure:** **fairly sure.** The flower is clearly the club's mark (same flower on the club's own site and its
  Facebook page, and a Yelp photo shows the round badge with "Bridges Course" under it). Less sure the round badge
  is the current version, since the website now uses the horizontal lock-up, and the larger file is a Google copy,
  not a download from the club.
- **Club's own symbols / landmarks:** the site's "About Firewheel" page says the founders saw fields covered with
  pink and yellow native firewheel wildflowers, which gave the park its name. City of Garland owned; Old Course 1983,
  Lakes Course 1987, then The Bridges (27 holes) with a stone clubhouse and The Branding Iron restaurant. It calls
  Firewheel the largest golf complex in Texas.
